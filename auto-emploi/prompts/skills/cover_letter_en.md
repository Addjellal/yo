# Skill — English cover letter

> This file is injected into the AI system prompt before every English cover
> letter generation. Edit it freely to refine the guidelines: changes apply to
> the next generation, no restart needed.

## Structure: results-first, three short paragraphs

1. **Hook** — open with a concrete, quantified achievement directly relevant
   to the company's stated need. No throat-clearing ("I am writing to apply
   for…"), no generic praise of the company.
2. **Proof** — 2 or 3 accomplishments from the resume that map to the role's
   top requirements. Each one follows the pattern: situation → action →
   measurable outcome (numbers, percentages, time saved, scale).
3. **Close** — one sentence on what the candidate will bring to THIS team,
   then a confident call to action proposing a conversation or interview.

## Tone and conventions

- Salutation: "Dear Hiring Manager," if no name is given; "Dear Ms./Mr. X,"
  if the offer names a contact.
- Sign-off: "Sincerely," or "Best regards," — nothing flowery.
- Active voice everywhere: "I led", "I shipped", "I reduced" — never
  "was responsible for" or "was involved in".
- Confident but factual. No hedging ("I believe I might be able to…"),
  no superlatives about oneself ("exceptional", "outstanding").
- Contractions are acceptable ("I'm", "I've") for a startup tone; avoid them
  for corporate or formal applications.

## Length and form

- 200 to 300 words, 3 paragraphs maximum. Recruiters skim: every sentence
  must earn its place.
- Sentences under 22 words on average. One idea per sentence.
- No bullet points in the letter body: connected prose only.
- Zero exclamation marks.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Restating the resume: the letter explains impact and fit, it does not list
  duties.
- Generic claims without evidence: "team player", "fast learner",
  "detail-oriented" — banned unless backed by a concrete example.
- Talking only about what the candidate wants ("this role would help me
  grow") instead of what they deliver.
- Apologizing for missing requirements; address gaps only if asked, and
  reframe around adjacent strengths.
- Inventing facts: every claim must exist in the provided resume.
- Weak closings ("I hope to hear from you") — replace with a direct proposal:
  "I would welcome the chance to discuss how I can help X achieve Y."

## ATS optimization (applicant tracking systems)

- Mirror the EXACT job title from the posting at least once, ideally in the
  first paragraph.
- Reuse the posting's key terms (technologies, methodologies, certifications)
  with their original spelling — only those genuinely supported by the resume.
- Spell out acronyms once if the posting does ("Search Engine Optimization
  (SEO)"), then use the acronym.
- Plain text only: no tables, no decorative characters, no unusual formatting.
